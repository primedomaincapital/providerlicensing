Provider licensing and credentialing timelines, costs, and market figures, by the numbers

Credentialing timelines, cost of delay, CAQH and NCQA figures, Interstate Medical Licensure Compact counts, and credentialing software funding — from published sources.

Three different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures the wait: how long licensing, credentialing, and payer enrollment take, and what each day of waiting costs. The second kind measures the machinery: the shared data portal, the verification standards, and the interstate compact that move the files. The third kind measures the market: the venture-funded companies building software to shorten the wait, and the domain sales that price health category names. The first kind tells a reader how expensive the problem is. The second tells a reader how the problem is organized. The third tells a reader who is paying to solve it.

What the latest numbers show is a market where the cost of waiting dwarfs the cost of the work itself: a clean commercial file takes 90 to 120 days, the delay costs about $10,122 per provider per month, and the companies automating the process have raised hundreds of millions of dollars — while the shared portal underneath it all is free for the provider and still leaves each payer to verify the same file again.

How to read these numbers

Included: credentialing and enrollment timelines from MGMA, AMA, DoctorsManagement, Helpware, and NexCure; the MGMA cost-of-delay figure via Becker's Hospital Review; Merritt Hawkins physician revenue via MD Revenue Group; outsourced credentialing price bands from CarePatron; CAQH portal figures and the 2019 CAQH practice survey via Sullivan McG; NCQA verification and recredentialing standards via Helpware; the Health Affairs 2009 practice-spending figure via the Commonwealth Fund summary; Interstate Medical Licensure Compact membership and volume from the Commission's FY2025 Annual Report and Year 9 Data Study; Medallion, Verifiable, CertifyOS, symplr, and Modio Health funding and scale from Forbes, PitchBook, Verifiable, Healthcare IT Today, Cooley, HIStalk, and CB Insights; and health domain sales from the published most-expensive-sales lists. Excluded: any credentialing cost presented as a single per-provider figure, since published sources measure the cost in parts; any company-claimed saving presented as a measured result, since CertifyOS's $2 billion and 75 percent figures are the company's own claims; and any funding valuation presented as a market price, since none of these companies is public.

Timelines are ranges for clean files, not promises — panel closures, state backlogs, and incomplete CAQH profiles push any of them further. The $10,122 monthly delay figure is an MGMA estimate of deferred revenue and access limitations, not a cash loss on every provider. Medicare allows limited retrospective billing (up to 30 days before the effective date under 42 CFR 424.521, up to 90 days after a Presidentially-declared disaster); commercial payers vary widely and many allow none. Forecasts and company claims are labeled as such, not as actuals.
