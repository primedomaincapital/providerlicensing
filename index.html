The thesis

A name for the paperwork that gates American medicine.

Before a clinician can treat a single insured patient, three separate gates have to open. The state has to license her. The hospital or health plan has to credential her — verifying education, training, and history against primary sources. And each payer has to enroll her, load her into its claims system, and give her an effective date. Until the last of those gates opens, the work is done and the bill cannot be sent.

The wait is measured in months, not days. A clean commercial file takes 90 to 120 days from submission to an effective date, and the Medical Group Management Association puts the full range at 90 to 180 days per payer, with the American Medical Association warning it can run to 180 days. Medicare enrollment through PECOS typically runs 60 to 90 days. Medicaid varies by state and can stretch further. A clinician who contracts with 19 payers — the average Medallion chief executive Derek Lo cites for the roughly 4 million credentialed providers in the United States — repeats the process 19 times, in 19 portals, against 19 deadlines.

The cost of the wait is not the filing fee. Outsourced credentialing runs $100 to $300 per payer per provider, and a full initial package commonly runs $2,500 to $5,000. The cost is the revenue that cannot be billed while the file sits in a queue: the Medical Group Management Association puts credentialing and enrollment delays at about $10,122 per provider, per month, in deferred revenue. A Merritt Hawkins survey of hospital chief financial officers found the average physician generates roughly $2.38 million in net revenue a year for an affiliated organization. Ninety days of waiting on one physician is a quarter of that, held up by forms.

Exact, and built for a funded buyer pool. The companies fixing this are venture-backed and nameable: Medallion, Verifiable, CertifyOS, symplr, and Modio Health, plus the credentialing outsourcers charging per payer application and the telehealth groups licensing clinicians in dozens of states at once. They all sell the same two words. This domain is those two words, in order, with nothing added.
